# Start from something worth learning

This is the design brief before PRIMM, not a replacement teaching sequence.
Use backward design: desired usable capability → evidence of independent use →
learning activities. Reference: Wiggins & McTighe, *Understanding by Design*,
[Backward Design](https://www.ascd.org/books/understanding-by-design-expanded-2nd-edition?chapter=backward-design).
Then teach with the Owner-selected Predict → Run → Investigate → Modify → Make.

## Before writing a lesson

Record six short answers in the ordinary author brief, not six learner panels:

1. Who is learning, what can they already do, and what do they not know yet?
2. What recognizable problem are they trying to solve? What do they currently do?
3. What makes that approach inconvenient? Could a simpler non-AI action solve it
   just as well? Reject invented need, not ordinary tasks.
4. What small useful result will they leave with? What decision remains theirs?
5. What changed input/purpose will show independent use without copying our prompt?
6. Which real case/source supports the capability or facts? Which material is a
   clearly labelled practice invention? Neither must impersonate the other.

A credible citation and a plausible story are not evidence of market demand.
Label proposed needs as hypotheses unless observed from real learners. Do not
promise measured time savings, mastery, retention or willingness to pay.

## Keep the learner's train of thought intact

Before a new action, connect its purpose to the current problem. Do not invent
a new problem just to fit an engine. Keep P/R on identical inputs. When changing
an example or audience, say what changed and why. After Run, inspect the actual
response; never assert an omission that this response may not contain. Give
necessary vocabulary/examples before the activity that uses them. Delete repeated
interface narration, not the explanation that makes the next action intelligible.

Observe the duration of meaningful actions during walkthrough. One or two minutes
is a pacing hypothesis, not a timer or word-count ceiling. Do not interrupt someone
writing. An action should yield a visible change, useful feedback or a new decision.
Attaching a file or clicking reveal is not evidence of independent understanding.

Check the action against the promised skill. If the lesson promises selecting
information for a particular person, a game that has already selected everything
and only rearranges the result is insufficient. Let the learner decide what to
keep, inspect the resulting artifact, and repair a missing or irrelevant detail.
Reordering is useful only after that decision, not a substitute for it. Keep this
check general; do not encode these five notices as the only allowed lesson design.

## Useful result, then transfer

Make changes a consequential input or purpose and withdraws the complete prompt.
Its visible requirements and private rubric must match. Evaluate the learner's
request and current edited result, not an obsolete generated draft. Do not require
magic phrases, professional vocabulary, extra facts or invisible word counts.
The ending keeps a usable result and one concrete next-use cue, not a mastery claim.
Review cards use changed situations, not just lists of prompting rules. Payment,
advertising, account flows and new telemetry are outside lesson authoring scope.

## Check the production method, not a favorite example

An independent Detector reads learner-visible order and verified facts, reporting
where purpose, vocabulary or transition breaks; it does not write replacement prose.
The Writer fixes, Flash polishes, the native CLI lands. Keep the design brief and
actual model/revision receipts beside the ordinary proposal. New-topic samples use
the same process without embedding these five stories in the skill. Five edited
lessons do not validate a thousand-lesson factory; demand and learning still need
real-user measurement.

When the visible task explicitly needs a concrete meeting/departure time, a PRIMM
Make `clockTimeCheck` may provide localized feedback for an absent clock expression.
This is only a deterministic missing-field precheck, never a correct-answer test:
14:00 and 下午两点 are accepted forms, but the time still has to match the source in
semantic evaluation. Do not enable it on tasks that only need a day or a duration.
