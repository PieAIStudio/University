# Why the teaching contract says what it says

The contract is short on purpose; this file carries the reasons, so a later
editor does not delete a rule whose cause they cannot see. Sources were read on
2026-09-19. They supply principles, not text: no course here copies another
course's wording, structure or examples, and free-to-read is not a licence.

## What the research gives us

| Principle | Source | Where it lands in the contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adults need to know **why** before they learn, and learn around problems they have, not subjects. | Knowles' andragogy ([overview](https://www.instructionaldesign.org/theories/andragogy/)) | §1 need first; §3 "why now" in every opening line |
| Instruction works when centred on a real task, activating what the learner already does, demonstrating, applying with feedback, then integrating into their own life. | Merrill, First Principles of Instruction ([summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Principles_of_Instruction)) | §1 "how do you do this without AI today"; §2 real case as demonstration; Make and Finish as integration |
| A prediction teaches only when the teacher helps reconcile it with what was observed. | White & Gunstone, Predict–Observe–Explain ([chapter](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/mono/10.4324/9780203761342-3/prediction-observation-explanation-richard-white-richard-gunstone)) | §3 `run.debrief`; F4 "silent after the result" |
| Whole tasks from the start, supportive information around them, procedural help just in time, support that fades. | van Merriënboer, 4C/ID ([overview PDF](https://www.4cid.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/vanmerrienboer-4cid-overview-of-main-design-principles-2021.pdf)) | Starter fully prepared → Modify fragments → Make with no scaffold |
| A task is a meaningful outcome in real life; the language focus comes **after** the task, on what the learner just used. | Willis, task-based learning ([ELT summary](https://www.onestopenglish.com/methodology-the-world-of-elt/teaching-approaches-task-based-learning/146502.article)) | Investigate explains after the operation (F5 "answer first") |
| Beginner AI courses for the public start from things people already meet (recommendations, voice, chat), move to what it is, then to cautions, without catastrophising. | Be Connected, "What is AI?" ([page](https://beconnected.esafety.gov.au/topic-library/articles-and-tips/what-is-ai)) | §0 no fear, no hype; §1 the learner's existing life first |
| PRIMM: predict, run, investigate, modify, make — from reading working code to writing your own. | PRIMM authors ([portal](https://primmportal.com/)) | The five-phase skeleton |

## What our own runs taught us (2026-09-16 → 2026-09-19)

These are failures seen in the pilot, promoted to rules. Each is now an F-code.

- **The need was invented to fit the material.** A licensed coffee photo became
  "describe your coffee to your family" — something nobody does, because they
  would just send the photo. Rule: need first; the "simpler way" elimination
  question (F1).
- **Predict could be answered by reading the request.** The request said "don't
  translate"; the question asked whether it would translate (F3).
- **The teacher went silent exactly when the learner needed interpretation.**
  After a live result, nothing connected it to the prediction. After the sort,
  the explanation had been on screen from the start (F4, F5).
- **Investigate drilled authored cards unrelated to the run.** A real
  transcription mis-heard one word, a perfect teaching moment, and the lesson
  instead sorted three pre-written cards whose text named their own bucket
  (F5, F15).
- **Material meta-text leaked into the AI's answer.** "This is a fictional
  practice notice" sat inside the text sent to AI, which dutifully repeated it
  (F12).
- **Product names without a gloss.** "Whisper", "Canvas", "ChatGPT Search"
  meant nothing to the learner; the case proved a launch happened, not that a
  person like them uses it (F7, §2).
- **Short is not clear.** The pilot's copy was trimmed hard and read as a string
  of disconnected assignments. The reading-level rule is about words and
  sentence length; the thread rule is about the steps between sentences.

## What this does not prove

Model reviewers, including the persona Detector, are plausibility checks. They
find where a reader would likely stop; they are not a person learning. Nothing
here claims retention, transfer or satisfaction until real learners have used
the lessons and their behaviour has been measured.
